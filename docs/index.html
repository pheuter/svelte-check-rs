Archived September 5, 2026. Source and releases remain available; no further fixes or releases are planned.

# How we got here

I started svelte-check-rs in January 2026, before official svelte-check supported TSGO or incremental compilation. On a large Svelte app, repeatedly waiting for checks slowed down development, CI, and coding agents.

A Rust parser, parallel transforms, native TypeScript checking, and persistent caches shortened that loop. Early measurements on my workloads showed order-of-magnitude improvements over the official checker at the time. That was the reason to build and share this project.

Since then, official svelte-check has added native checking and incremental caches. Its implementation explicitly credits svelte-check-rs and svelte-fast-check as inspirations. It is rewarding to see those benefits become part of the official tooling.

Rust retains a speed advantage in our final benchmarks, but the practical gap has narrowed. Maintaining a separate parser, transformer, source mappings, and ecosystem integrations no longer makes sense for that remaining benefit. We recommend official svelte-check going forward.

# Moving forward

Replace the svelte-check-rs development dependency and commands in your scripts, CI, and agent instructions with official svelte-check. Keep your existing SvelteKit sync step and review flags against upstream’s current documentation.

For native checking and caching, upstream offers --tsgo --incremental. Follow its TypeScript dependency instructions and check the documented limitations, including Svelte files outside the tsconfig root. The newer --tsgo-experimental-api mode has different tradeoffs and is experimental.

Run the official checker and review its diagnostics before relying on the migrated command. The tools do not always report the same results; our real-application benchmark records that difference.

The archived repository preserves the source, releases, and technical documentation under the MIT license.
